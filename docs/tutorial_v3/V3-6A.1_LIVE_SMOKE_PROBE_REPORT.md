## Final Gate Verdict

# FAIL

**Winning prompt variant: NONE.**

Twelve guideline images were generated live (4 categories × 3 prompt variants)
and every one was inspected pixel-by-pixel. All 12 requests returned valid image
bytes — the API contract works perfectly. The gate fails on **behaviour**.

The blocking failure is reproducible across **all three variants**:

> **Finished makeup from IMAGE 2 leaks onto IMAGE 1 in regions that are not the
> current category.**

A Foundation step came back with eyeshadow, eyeliner and a more saturated lip.
A Lip Colour step came back with cheek blush the original does not have. An
Eyeliner step came back with cheek blush. This is exactly the failure mode
Source of Truth §6 and §14 forbid, and V3-6 §21 lists as blocking.

A second, independent failure: **Blush placement contradicted the Step Spec in
all three variants.**

## 3. Variant 1 Findings — roles stated once, in the preamble

Overlay vocabulary was immediately good: translucent zones, arrows, paths, no
typography. Identity was preserved in all four. But:

- **Foundation** gained eyeshadow, eyeliner and a more saturated lip versus the
  original, and its white zone read as a pale foundation several shades light.
- **Lip Colour** gained cheek flush. Its blue overlay, however, was the single
  clearest "this is a diagram, not makeup" signal of the entire run.
- **Blush** zones sat low and medial — under-eye, extending toward the nose —
  when the Step Spec says *upper outer cheeks, starting outside the pupil line*.
- **Eyeliner** was the strongest result: correct lash-line path, outer-corner
  arrows, no finished liner, minimal drift.

---

## 4. Variant 2 Findings — role notes adjacent to each image + non-photographic framing

**Intent:** reduce bleed by moving the role statements next to their image parts
and reframing overlays as a diagram layer.

**Outcome: overlay quality improved; bleed did not improve, and blush regressed.**

- **Foundation** — arrows became clean line art, and eye/lip exclusions were
  drawn explicitly. But the zone became *more* opaque, reading as a sheet-mask/
  pale base, eye and lip drift persisted, and a faint glyph artifact appeared
  near the right temple.
- **Blush** — **regression.** The two cheek zones collapsed into a single wide
  band spanning both cheeks *across the bridge of the nose* — anatomically wrong
  for blush and further from the Step Spec than variant 1.
- **Eyeliner** — path and arrows still good, identity preserved, but cheek flush
  now clearly transferred from IMAGE 2.
- **Lip Colour** — overlay improved to properly translucent with a visible lip
  underneath; but cheek flush became *more* pronounced than variant 1, and eye
  definition drifted.

---

## 5. Variant 3 Findings — explicit cosmetic-colour-transfer ban

**Intent:** forbid sampling or approximating any makeup colour from IMAGE 2.

**Outcome: solved "overlay looks like product"; did not solve bleed.**

- **Foundation** — **best single image of the run.** The coverage zone rendered
  in mint/teal: unmistakably a diagram layer, impossible to confuse with
  foundation. Arrows correct, eye/lip exclusions correct, no typography.
  **But eye and lip drift persisted anyway.**
- **Blush** — zones still low and medial. Step Spec placement failed a third time.
- **Eyeliner** — good path and arrows; cheek flush still transferred.
- **Lip Colour** — the overlay *desaturated the actual lips* (greying them out)
  rather than sitting on top, which is itself an alteration of IMAGE 1; cheek
  flush still present; faint glyph-like marks inside the lip zone.

The colour-transfer ban demonstrably changed overlay *rendering* — that is why
foundation turned teal — so the instruction was received and acted on. It simply
did not stop the model from redrawing the rest of the face closer to the target.

## 7. Identity Preservation — **PASS across all 12**

This is the clearest positive result. In every image the subject remains
recognisably the same person: face proportions, bone structure, nose, jaw,
hair including stray strands, expression, gaze, framing, lighting and background
all survive. **There was no identity replacement, no face swap, and no wholesale
blending of the two portraits.** The catastrophic failure mode this gate was
built to detect did not occur.

The alterations observed are cosmetic-surface only — added blush, added eye
definition, shifted lip saturation, and (variant 3, lips) desaturation.

---

## 8. Makeup Transfer — **FAIL, the decisive finding**

Present in all three variants and in three of four categories. The pattern is
consistent: the model treats IMAGE 2 as a *style target for the whole face*
rather than as reference for one category. It draws the requested overlay
correctly **and also** nudges the rest of the face toward the finished look.

Escalating the prompt did not fix it. Variant 3's overlays became emphatically
non-cosmetic (teal) while the face underneath simultaneously carried transferred
eye and lip makeup — the model separated "overlay colour" from "face colour" as
instructed, then applied target makeup to the face anyway.

---

## 9. Step Spec Alignment — mixed, with one hard failure

Foundation, Eyeliner and Lip Colour followed WHERE / DIRECTION / TECHNIQUE
faithfully: centre-outward arrows, lash-line path with an outer-corner lift,
centre-to-corner lip fill with a border outline.

**Blush failed all three times.** The Step Spec is explicit — *upper outer
cheeks, starting outside the pupil line, blending upward and outward toward the
temples*. Every variant placed the zone low and medial, under the eye and
reaching toward the nose. Variant 2 was worst, merging both zones across the
nose bridge. The arrows were consistently correct; the **zone anchor point** was
consistently wrong.

---

## 10. Target Reference Behaviour

IMAGE 2 was **not** copied photographically, **not** substituted for IMAGE 1, and
**not** blended at the identity level — genuine positives. But it was also not
isolated to the current category. It behaved as a whole-face style reference,
which is precisely the isolation this architecture depends on.

---

## 11. Practical Visual Usefulness — the one strong result

Where the model was left alone to draw, output quality was genuinely good:
clean translucent zones, legible directional arrows, circular blending markers
matching "soft circular blending", a fine lash-line path with a wing marker, and
a lip border outline with fill arrows. **No output depended on AI-rendered
words**, aside from two faint glyph artifacts.

A user could act on the Foundation, Eyeliner and Lip Colour visuals. The Blush
visuals would teach the wrong placement.

## 15. Recommendation for V3-6B

# BLOCKED — DO NOT START V3-6B

Building the production pipeline now would wire up a generator that reliably
paints target makeup onto regions the current step is not teaching. A user on
the Foundation step would see themselves already wearing eyeliner and lipstick —
which defeats the entire premise of a step-by-step tutorial and violates the
central V3 invariant.

Per V3-6A.1 §17, I have **not** changed the architecture in response. The
following all require an explicit product/engineering decision and were
deliberately not attempted:

1. **Remove IMAGE 2**, grounding the target through `targetLookCues` text only.
   Cheapest test of the hypothesis that two-image grounding causes the bleed —
   but §23 makes the canonical preview central to V3, so dropping it is a
   product decision, not an engineering shortcut.
2. **Crop IMAGE 2 to the current category** (send only the cheek region for a
   blush step) so there is no eye or lip makeup available to transfer. Preserves
   two-image grounding; needs a non-generative crop, which Source of Truth §17
   already anticipates.
3. **Accept and mask the bleed** — treat the guideline as showing "the look in
   progress". This weakens the no-finished-makeup rule and I do not recommend it.
4. **Try a different image model** against this same gate.
5. **Post-process** — composite the model's overlay onto the untouched original
   client-side, discarding whatever it did to the face. Structurally guarantees
   identity and no-transfer, but needs overlay/photo separation the model does
   not currently provide.

Option 2 is the most promising: it keeps the architecture intact and directly
removes the material being transferred. The blush placement failure is separate
and would need Step Spec wording work regardless.
